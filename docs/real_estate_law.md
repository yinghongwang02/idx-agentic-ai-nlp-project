# California Real Estate Law and Disclosure Reference

This project-maintained reference summarizes selected California real estate
law and disclosure concepts relevant to the IDX Exchange knowledge RAG
assistant.

The summaries are based primarily on California Department of Real Estate
materials and cited California statutes.

This document is intended for informational RAG grounding only. It is not legal
advice and is not a complete statement of California real estate law.

Last reviewed: August 2026

---

## Transfer Disclosure Statement — TDS

California law requires a Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) for covered
transfers of residential real property, subject to statutory exceptions.

The TDS is completed by the seller and provides disclosures concerning the
physical condition of the property and known material defects or conditions.

California Department of Real Estate guidance also explains that the buyer's
real estate agent has responsibilities related to visually inspecting the
property and disclosing defects that are readily observable.

Sources:
- California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "First Time Home Buyers — Disclosures"

## Material Facts Affecting Property Value or Desirability

California disclosure obligations are not limited to information appearing on
a standard disclosure form.

California Civil Code § 1102.1 preserves existing disclosure obligations
concerning facts materially affecting the value and desirability of real
property.

Accordingly, completing a statutory disclosure form does not by itself replace
other applicable duties to disclose material facts.

Source:
- California Civil Code § 1102.1
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "Real Estate Law — Pertinent Excerpts from the California Codes"

## Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement — NHDS

California law requires specified natural-hazard information to be disclosed
for covered real-property transfers.

The Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement addresses whether property is located
within specified hazard areas identified by California law.

The statutory disclosure includes the following hazard categories:

1. Special Flood Hazard Area
2. Area of Potential Flooding from dam failure
3. High or Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone
4. Wildland Area that may contain substantial forest fire risks and hazards
5. Earthquake Fault Zone
6. Seismic Hazard Zone

A seller or seller's agent may use a third-party consultant to provide natural
hazard disclosure information.

Using a third-party consultant does not remove the applicable obligation to
deliver the required Natural Hazard Disclosure Statement to the buyer.

Sources:
- California Civil Code §§ 1103–1103.4
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "Disclosures in Real Property Transactions"

## Agency Relationship Disclosure

California real estate transactions can require written disclosure of the
agency relationships involved in the transaction.

The Agency Relationship Disclosure identifies whom the real estate agent
represents, such as:

- the seller,
- the buyer, or
- both the seller and buyer as a dual agent.

California law permits an agent to represent both the seller and buyer in a
transaction only with the knowledge and consent of both parties.

Agents also owe duties associated with their agency relationships, including
applicable fiduciary and disclosure duties.

Sources:
- California Civil Code §§ 2079.13–2079.24
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "First Time Home Buyers — Disclosures"
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "Disclosures in Real Property Transactions"

---

## Dual Agency

Dual agency occurs when a real estate agent represents both the seller and the
buyer in the same transaction.

In California, dual agency requires disclosure of the relationship and the
knowledge and consent of both the seller and buyer.

A dual agent owes duties to both parties and is subject to restrictions on
disclosing certain confidential negotiating information without the relevant
party's permission.

Sources:
- California Civil Code §§ 2079.13–2079.24
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "Disclosures in Real Property Transactions"

## Preliminary Title Report — Transaction Document

A preliminary title report is a transaction document prepared from a title
company's search of information concerning a property.

California Department of Real Estate consumer guidance describes the report as
identifying the ownership history of the property and liens or encumbrances
affecting it.

The preliminary title report is included here as an important transaction
document; it should not be interpreted as equivalent to a statutory seller
disclosure requirement such as the TDS or NHDS.

Source:
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "First Time Home Buyers — Disclosures"

## Visual Inspection and Disclosure

California real estate licensees can have inspection and disclosure duties in
residential transactions.

California Department of Real Estate consumer guidance explains that the
buyer's real estate agent is responsible for conducting a visual inspection of
the property and disclosing defects that are readily observed.

California real estate law also addresses a broker's duty to conduct a
reasonably competent and diligent visual inspection in applicable residential
transactions and to disclose material facts revealed by that inspection.

Sources:
- California Civil Code § 2079
- California Department of Real Estate,
  "First Time Home Buyers — Disclosures"

## Important Limitation

This document contains selected summaries for retrieval and educational use.

California real estate disclosure and agency requirements can depend on the
property, transaction, parties, statutory exceptions, and current law.

For authoritative requirements, consult current California statutes and
California Department of Real Estate materials. Legal questions should be
directed to an appropriately qualified legal professional.

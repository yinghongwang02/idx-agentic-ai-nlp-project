# Real Estate Knowledge Guide

## Days on Market (DOM)

Days on Market, commonly abbreviated as DOM, measures the number of days a property has been listed for sale before it goes under contract or is removed from the market, depending on the data source.

A lower DOM can indicate that homes are selling relatively quickly, while a higher DOM can indicate that properties are taking longer to sell. DOM should be interpreted together with other market indicators rather than used alone.

## Sale-to-List Ratio

The sale-to-list ratio compares a property's final sale price with its most recent listing price.

A ratio of 100% means the property sold at its listing price. A ratio below 100% means the final sale price was below the listing price, while a ratio above 100% means the property sold above the listing price.

For example, an average sale-to-list ratio of 98% means properties sold for approximately 98% of their listed prices on average.

## Price per Square Foot

Price per square foot, often abbreviated as PPSF, divides a property's price by its living area.

PPSF can help compare homes of different sizes within similar locations and property types. It should not be treated as a complete valuation because condition, lot size, location, amenities, and other characteristics can also affect value.

## Comparable Sales

Comparable sales, or comps, are recently sold properties used as reference points when evaluating another property or a local market.

Useful comparable properties generally share important characteristics with the target property, such as location, property type, size, bedroom and bathroom count, and recent sale timing.

Comparable sales can provide evidence about market pricing, price per square foot, typical days on market, and sale-to-list behavior.

## Listing Price and Sale Price

The listing price is the price at which a property is offered for sale. The sale price is the final price recorded when the transaction closes.

The difference between listing price and sale price can provide information about negotiation outcomes and market competitiveness.

## Market Trend Indicators

Real-estate market trends can be evaluated by comparing recent market metrics with an earlier period.

Useful indicators include changes in median sale price, median price per square foot, average days on market, and average sale-to-list ratio.

Increasing prices or sale-to-list ratios may indicate stronger seller conditions, while increasing days on market may indicate slower demand. Multiple indicators should be considered together before describing a market as warming, cooling, or stable.

## Structured Property Search

Structured property search converts explicit buyer requirements into filters over listing attributes.

Examples include city, maximum price, minimum bedrooms, minimum bathrooms, and property type.

Structured search is useful when a user's requirements can be represented directly by listing fields.

## Semantic Similarity

Semantic similarity compares the meaning and descriptive characteristics of properties using vector embeddings.

A property's structured attributes and descriptive text can be represented as an embedding vector. Similar vectors can help identify properties with related characteristics even when their descriptions do not use exactly the same words.

## Hybrid Home Recommendation

Hybrid recommendation combines multiple similarity signals rather than relying on a single retrieval method.

A hybrid real-estate recommendation system may combine structured property similarity with semantic embedding similarity. Sold comparable information can then provide additional market evidence for candidate properties.

This approach allows recommendations to consider both explicit property characteristics and descriptive similarity.

## Negotiation Signals

Negotiation signals are market or listing characteristics that may help a buyer understand potential negotiating conditions.

Examples include days on market, sale-to-list behavior of comparable properties, a property's price position relative to comparable sales, and recurring ownership costs such as association fees.

These signals provide decision support rather than a guarantee of a particular negotiation outcome.
